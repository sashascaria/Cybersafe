Online Privacy

Not everything about you needs to be online. Here is what to keep to yourself.

What is personal information?

Personal information is anything that could be used to identify you or find you in real life. This includes your full name, address, school, phone number, and date of birth. It also includes photos that show where you are, like your school uniform or your house in the background.

Why does sharing too much cause problems?

Once something is posted online, you lose control of it. Other people can screenshot it, share it, or use it against you. Strangers can use your personal details to find out where you live or go to school. Even something small like telling people what time you leave for school can be dangerous if the wrong person reads it.

Check your social media settings

Most social media apps are set to public by default, which means anyone in the world can see what you post. Go into your settings and make your profile private so only people you approve can follow you. Also check which apps on your phone have permission to access your location, camera, or microphone. If an app does not need it, turn it off.

Think before you post

Before you post anything online, ask yourself: would I be happy for my school or parents to see this? If the answer is no, do not post it. The internet has a long memory and things that seem funny or harmless now can cause problems years later. Universities and employers sometimes check social media too.

Your privacy matters

Sharing personal information online can lead to identity theft, stalking, and targeted scams. Taking a few simple steps now can protect you from a lot of problems later.

Keep these private

  • Your full name and address
  • Your school name
  • Your phone number
  • Your location in real time
  • Photos showing where you live
  • Your date of birth
