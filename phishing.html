Phishing

Scammers send fake emails and messages to steal your info. Here is how to spot them.

What is phishing?

Phishing is when someone pretends to be a trusted company or person to trick you into giving them your personal information. They usually do this through emails or messages that look real but are actually fake. The goal is to get you to click a link, log in to a fake website, or give them your password.

What do phishing messages look like?

They often pretend to be from places like Netflix, your bank, Amazon, or even your school. The message usually says something urgent like "Your account has been suspended" or "You have won a prize, click here." The email address might look almost right but have something slightly off, like "support@netfl1x.com" instead of netflix.com.

How to check if a link is safe

Before you click any link in an email, hover over it with your mouse and look at what address actually appears. If it looks strange or different from the website it claims to be, do not click it. You can also go directly to the official website by typing the address yourself rather than clicking the link.

What to do if you think it is phishing

Do not click any links or download any attachments. Report the message as spam or phishing in your email app. If you already clicked something and entered your details, change your password straight away and let a parent or teacher know. It is better to be safe and tell someone than to keep it to yourself.

Why phishing is so dangerous

Phishing is one of the most common cyber attacks. It works because the messages look so real. Even adults fall for them, so knowing the warning signs really matters.

Warning signs

  • Strange or misspelled email address
  • Creates panic or urgency
  • Asks for passwords or card details
  • Links do not match the website name
  • Bad spelling or grammar
  • Unexpected attachments
