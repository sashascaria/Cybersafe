💬 Cyberbullying

Being cruel online is still bullying. Here is what to do if it happens to you.

What is cyberbullying?

Cyberbullying is when someone uses technology to repeatedly hurt, threaten, or embarrass another person. It can happen on social media, in gaming chats, over text, or through group messages. It includes sending mean messages, sharing embarrassing photos without permission, spreading rumours online, and deliberately leaving someone out of group chats.

Why is it so harmful?

Unlike face-to-face bullying, cyberbullying can happen at any time including at home, when you should feel safe. Hurtful messages can be seen and shared by many people quickly. The person being bullied can feel like there is no escape from it. This can seriously affect mental health, confidence, and school performance.

What to do if someone is cyberbullying you

Do not respond to the person, as that can make things worse. Screenshot everything as evidence. Block the person on the platform where it is happening. Then tell a trusted adult, like a parent or teacher. You do not have to deal with it on your own. Reporting it is not weak, it is the right thing to do.

Being a good bystander

If you see someone being bullied online, do not join in, even if you think it is just a joke. You can support the person by sending them a private message to let them know you are there for them. You can also report the post or comment directly to the platform. Small actions make a real difference.

You are not alone

Cyberbullying is more common than many people think. If it happens to you, it is not your fault and there is always someone who can help.

What to do

  • Do not respond to the bully
  • Screenshot everything
  • Block the person
  • Report it to the platform
  • Tell a trusted adult
  • Support friends who are targeted
