️ Online Scams

If something online seems too good to be true, it almost always is.

What is an online scam?

An online scam is when someone tricks you into giving them money, personal information, or access to your accounts. Scams can happen through social media, text messages, email, gaming platforms, and even fake websites. They are designed to look completely real, which is why so many people fall for them.

Common types of scams

Some of the most common scams include fake prize messages ("You have won an iPhone, click here to claim!"), free game currency offers, fake celebrity giveaways, and requests from strangers online who claim to need money urgently. There are also tech support scams where someone pretends to be from a company like Apple or Microsoft and says your device has a virus.

Gaming scams to watch out for

Gaming scams are really common for young people. Someone might offer you free Robux, V-Bucks, or skins in exchange for your login details. Once you hand over your username and password, they take over your account. No legitimate game will ever ask for your password. If someone is offering free in-game currency, it is almost certainly a scam.

What to do if you think you are being scammed

Stop communicating with the person straight away. Do not send any money or give any personal information. Screenshot the messages as evidence, then tell a parent or trusted adult. If you have already been scammed, your parents can contact the relevant platform or bank to try to get help.

Scams target young people too

Young people are actually a common target for scammers because they are often more trusting and less experienced with these tricks. Knowing what to look for makes a big difference.

Red flags for scams

  • Too good to be true offers
  • Pressure to act quickly
  • Requests for your password
  • Strangers asking for money
  • Free prizes for clicking a link
  • Unusual payment methods asked for
