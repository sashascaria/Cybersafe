Passwords

A weak password is like leaving your front door wide open. Here is how to make a good one.

What makes a password weak?

Most people use passwords like their name, birthday, or "password123". These are way too easy to guess. Hackers use programs that can try millions of combinations in seconds, so short or obvious passwords get cracked really fast.

What makes a password strong?

A strong password is long (at least 12 characters), uses a mix of letters, numbers, and symbols, and does not include any real words or personal details. One good trick is to use a passphrase. Pick 4 random words and join them together, like "CloudPurpleBoatFish7!". It is long, random, and easy to remember.

Never reuse passwords

If you use the same password on every site and one of those sites gets hacked, the attackers now have access to all your accounts. Always use a different password for each account. A password manager app can help you keep track of them all without having to remember each one.

Turn on two-factor authentication

Two-factor authentication (2FA) means even if someone knows your password, they still cannot get in without a second code sent to your phone. Most apps and websites have this in their security settings. Turn it on wherever you can, especially for email and social media.

Why passwords matter

Over 80% of hacking cases involve weak or stolen passwords. A strong password is the first and easiest thing you can do to protect yourself.

Top tips

  • Use at least 12 characters
  • Mix letters, numbers and symbols
  • Never use your name or birthday
  • Use a different password per site
  • Use a password manager
  • Turn on 2FA wherever possible
